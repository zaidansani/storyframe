# 4.1 What is cellular respiration?

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to
release energy, stored in the form of **ATP**^[Adenosine triphosphate, the primary energy carrier used by cells.] (adenosine triphosphate). It
happens in three main stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron
transport chain.^[The electron transport chain is the final and most productive stage, generating the bulk of a cell's ATP.]

The overall reaction can be summarized as:

```
C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy (ATP)
```

Most of this process takes place inside the mitochondria, often called the
"powerhouse of the cell" — though glycolysis itself actually happens in the
cytoplasm, before the pyruvate produced ever reaches the mitochondria.

# 4.2 The three stages

Each stage contributes differently to the cell's total ATP output:

| Stage                    | Location                     | ATP yield (approx.) |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Glycolysis               | Cytoplasm                    | 2 ATP               |
| Krebs cycle              | Mitochondrial matrix         | 2 ATP               |
| Electron transport chain | Inner mitochondrial membrane | ~34 ATP             |

    answer={[1, 2]}
    explanation="The Krebs cycle and electron transport chain both occur inside the mitochondria — glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm, and fermentation is an anaerobic alternative pathway that doesn't use the mitochondria at all."

Notice how the electron transport chain produces by far the most ATP. This
is why cells that need lots of energy, like muscle cells, are packed with
mitochondria.
